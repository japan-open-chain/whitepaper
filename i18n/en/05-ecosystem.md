# 5. Ecosystem and Partnerships

## 5.1 Projects on Japan Open Chain

Various projects are already running on Japan Open Chain. By its nature, Japan Open Chain suits projects that must comply with Japanese law, such as those in finance and local government, and work in those fields is advancing.

## 5.2 Stablecoin Projects

Since June 2023, the revised Payment Services Act has made it possible to issue stablecoins under Japanese law. Following this revision, a stablecoin project led by G.U. Technologies, one of the Validators, is running proof-of-concept trials to issue stablecoins on Japan Open Chain together with Aozora Bank, Minna Bank (the digital bank of Fukuoka Financial Group), Kiraboshi Bank, Shikoku Bank, and others. Overseas financial institutions have also begun considering issuing stablecoins on Japan Open Chain.

Stablecoins whose value is backed by banks are still almost unprecedented worldwide, and the project has drawn global attention, including coverage by the international edition of CoinDesk, an authoritative crypto-asset media outlet. As the project progresses, payments — such as interbank transfers that now cost hundreds of yen (thousands for international remittances) and credit-card payments that charge a few percent — could be made for less than one yen, bringing major change to the financial world.

Beyond this, the web3 world is said to be heading toward "programmable money" that can be programmed and moved freely. Money could be paid automatically when certain conditions are met, currencies could be exchanged on the blockchain without an exchange, and AI could manage funds on your behalf.

The stablecoin market reached about USD 280 billion as of November 2026 ([2.4](02-market.md#24-growth-of-the-stablecoin-market)) and is forecast to keep growing substantially. Japan Open Chain aims to become one of the core infrastructures for it.

## 5.3 NFT Projects

E-residency is drawing attention as a way to advance digital transformation in local government. Cogear Inc., a Sony Group subsidiary, is partnering with Kaga City, Ishikawa Prefecture, on a proof-of-concept e-residency program using NFTs issued on Japan Open Chain.

E-residency lets people who do not live in a municipality hold its e-residency membership card and receive some of its services or discounts at local shops. At the national level, Estonia drew attention by issuing e-citizenship.

As remote work spreads, people are expected to live across regions, and lifestyles that move between places without a fixed residence may become common. E-residency will be one of the systems that supports this.

Japan Open Chain has also been adopted for NFT issuance in Japan Post's initiative "Promoting regional attractions through the 'Post Office of the Future'", and partnerships with several NFT-issuance solution companies are progressing as Development Partners. We expect many kinds of NFTs to be issued going forward.

## 5.4 Collaboration with Global Projects

Japan Open Chain is pursuing partnerships not only in Japan but with overseas node providers, cross-chain protocols, crypto-asset exchanges, DeFi projects, and others. Supported projects will be announced on the website or social media as they are confirmed.

## 5.5 Other

Use in finance, supply chain, real estate, entertainment, education, and other fields is under consideration, and several projects are already under way. Each will be announced on the website or social media once it can be disclosed.

Through partnerships with companies in many fields, Japan Open Chain will contribute to spreading web3 technology in Japan and around the world.
